> A 29-year-old woman presents with intermittent palpitations, heat intolerance, and unintentional weight loss. Physical exam reveals a fine tremor, tachycardia, and a diffusely enlarged, non-tender thyroid gland. Laboratory tests show a suppressed TSH (<0.01 mIU/L), elevated free T4, and positive TSH receptor antibodies, and a thyroid ultrasound shows diffuse enlargement with increased blood flow and no nodules. An endocrinologist diagnosed Graves' disease last week; she has not yet started antithyroid medication and has never received radioactive iodine or thyroid surgery. She has mild upper eyelid retraction but no eye pain, double vision, or bulging of the eyes. Other blood tests show ALT 28 U/L, AST 25 U/L, creatinine 62 µmol/L, and a white blood cell count of 6.1 × 10⁹/L, and hepatitis B surface antigen is negative. A urine pregnancy test is negative; she uses a copper intrauterine device and has no plans to become pregnant. She works as an elementary school teacher and takes no other regular medications.
