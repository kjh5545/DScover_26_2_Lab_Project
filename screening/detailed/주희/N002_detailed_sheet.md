> A 24-year-old man with asthma since childhood presents with worsening wheezing, chest tightness, and night-time cough despite daily inhaled corticosteroid use. A pulmonologist diagnosed asthma when he was 8 years old, and he has no other lung disease. He uses high-dose budesonide-formoterol twice daily, and his pulmonologist has scheduled his first dupilumab injection for next week as part of standard care. Ten days ago he visited the emergency department for an asthma attack after exposure to a cat; he had no fever, his chest X-ray was normal, and he was treated with a 5-day course of oral prednisone without antibiotics. Spirometry two weeks ago met ATS quality criteria and showed an FEV1 of 68% of predicted, and his blood eosinophil count is 450 cells/μL. He has never smoked cigarettes, vaped, or used cigars or marijuana. His BMI is 24 kg/m², and his resting oxygen saturation is 97% on room air. He works as a graphic designer.
