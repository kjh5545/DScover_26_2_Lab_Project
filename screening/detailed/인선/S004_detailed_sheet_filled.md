> A 68-year-old man with a long smoking history presents with painless gross hematuria. CT urography reveals a mass in the bladder wall. He has smoked about one pack a day for 45 years and is still smoking. The hematuria began three weeks ago and has been intermittent. Cystoscopy two days ago revealed a papillary bladder tumor on the left bladder wall, suspicious for urothelial carcinoma. Transurethral resection of bladder tumor (TURBT) is scheduled for next week. No histopathological diagnosis has been established yet. CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis shows no enlarged lymph nodes or evidence of distant metastases. He is fully active, walks about three miles most mornings, and has no restrictions in daily or strenuous activity. Serum creatinine is 1.0 mg/dL (institutional upper limit of normal: 1.3 mg/dL). Urinalysis shows red blood cells but no white blood cells or nitrite, and a urine culture was negative. He takes amlodipine 5 mg daily for hypertension and atorvastatin 20 mg daily for hyperlipidemia.
