> A 60-year-old woman presents with progressive dyspnea, dry cough, and clubbing of the fingers. Chest CT reveals bilateral basal reticular opacities with honeycombing. She was diagnosed with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis by her pulmonologist 14 months ago, and her breathlessness has worsened gradually over the past 18 months without any sudden change. She is followed in the outpatient clinic and has not been hospitalized for her lung disease. High-resolution CT shows a usual interstitial pneumonia pattern with fibrosis predominating and only minimal emphysema, and her ANA is negative. Pulmonary function tests show FVC 68% of predicted and hemoglobin-corrected DLCO 42% of predicted. She has taken nintedanib 150 mg twice daily at the same dose for the past eight months. An echocardiogram last month showed normal left ventricular function. She is postmenopausal and a former smoker (20 pack-years, quit 10 years ago). She also takes omeprazole 20 mg daily for reflux and levothyroxine 75 mcg daily for hypothyroidism.
